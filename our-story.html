Empowering children through financial education.

How it began

One small gullak. One big idea.

Why a clay piggy bank became a movement — and how a simple question about children and money turned into Project Gullak.

The founding story

It started with a simple thought: every child deserves to understand money before they have to make big decisions about it.

We kept thinking about the little things we learn as children — saving a coin in a gullak, choosing between two things, waiting for something we really want. These tiny moments quietly teach us lessons that stay with us.

But for many children, especially those growing up with limited resources, there are fewer opportunities to turn these little lessons into practical financial knowledge.

So, we decided to start with something small — a few conversations, a few activities, and a simple idea.

And that little idea became Project Gullak.

Because sometimes, a small beginning can hold the promise of a much bigger future.

Where we're going

A gullak wherever children gather.

Our goal is simple: make financial education a normal part of growing up — not a lucky accident. We don't hand over a lesson plan and hope for the best. We run the sessions ourselves, travelling to orphanages and schools to work directly with the children. Wherever we're invited, we arrive with the gullaks, the activities and the full session — and it stays free for every child.

Why "Gullak"?

A gullak — the little clay piggy bank — is the first money teacher most of us ever had.

It teaches patience, and the quiet thrill of watching savings grow, one coin at a time. We put that feeling at the centre of everything we teach.

What we believe

A child who learns to save is a child who learns to plan — and a child who plans can build anything.

— The Project Gullak belief
